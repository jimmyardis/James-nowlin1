How buying a home works in South Carolina

Buying a home in South Carolina usually takes 30 to 45 days from accepted offer to closing. You'll get pre-approved, sign a buyer agreement, make an offer with a due diligence period, inspect the home, and close with a licensed attorney, then apply for the 4% primary residence tax rate.

  1. Get pre-approved

    Talk to a lender before you tour. A pre-approval tells you what you can borrow and makes your offers credible. James can recommend local lenders and help you understand the numbers, from rate to closing costs.

  2. Sign a buyer agreement

    Before touring together, you and James sign a written agreement that spells out his services and how he's paid. Compensation is negotiable, and he'll walk you through every line.

  3. Find the right home

    Set up a search across every MLS listing in the Midlands. James checks each home's county, tax rate and school assignment so you compare true monthly costs.

  4. Make an offer

    James writes the offer on South Carolina's standard contract forms, with a due diligence period, fee and earnest money set to fit the home and the competition.

  5. Use your due diligence period

    Schedule the home inspection, termite letter, survey and insurance quote right away. If something isn't right, you can negotiate or walk away during this period.

  6. Appraisal and loan approval

    Your lender orders the appraisal and finishes underwriting. James tracks these deadlines alongside the contract dates.

  7. Close with an attorney

    South Carolina requires an attorney to handle the closing. You'll do a final walk-through, sign the documents and get your keys.

  8. Apply for the 4% assessment

    After closing, apply with your county assessor for the 4% legal residence rate on your primary home. You'll need an SC driver's license and vehicle registration showing the new address, so movers from out of state should handle those first. The resources page lists every step and link.

Buyer questions

Do I need an attorney to buy a house in South Carolina?

Yes. South Carolina requires a licensed attorney to conduct real estate closings. The attorney handles the title search, prepares the closing documents and disburses funds. As the buyer, you usually choose the closing attorney, and James can recommend a few to compare.

What is a due diligence period in South Carolina?

It's a set number of days after your offer is accepted when you can inspect the home, check the survey, insurance and neighborhood, and walk away for any reason. Many SC contracts also include a non-refundable due diligence fee paid to the seller. The length and fee are negotiable, and James helps you set both to fit the home and the market.

Do I have to sign an agreement with a buyer's agent?

Yes, in most cases. Since August 2024, agents who use the MLS need a written agreement with a buyer before touring homes together. The agreement spells out what the agent does and how they're paid, and the compensation is negotiable. James walks through it with you before your first showing.

How much do I need for a down payment?

It depends on the loan. VA and USDA loans can require no down payment, FHA loans start at 3.5%, and some conventional loans start at 3%. You'll also need money for the due diligence fee, earnest money, inspections and closing costs. Down payment assistance is available through SC Housing for qualifying buyers. A lender can give you exact numbers, and James can recommend a few.

How long does it take to close on a house in SC?

About 30 to 45 days with financing is typical, and two to three weeks is common for cash. The timeline depends on the lender, the appraisal and the due diligence period you negotiate.
